On My Mission (Brightly Beams)


I had no idea what I was getting into three years ago. That's when I accepted the call from a prophet of God to deliver the good news of Christ to those living in another country. Just in case you don’t know what a mission for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints is like (other denominations may call it a mission trip, but I will not call it that because it is an entirely different animal), it is a two-year, social and physical and emotional sprint. This sprint is sometimes spent actually sprinting, but includes every task imaginable in helping teach people more about the gospel of Jesus Christ. That’s how it felt for me, at least. That being the case, I cannot possibly tell you everything that happened in those two years. Because of that, I can’t really remember that much of it either. But I do remember thinking about a certain hymn quite often.
The hymn, “Brightly Beams our Father’s Mercy,” is not translated into Thai, the language of the place where I served my mission. It wasn’t something that I could truly share with these people that I was trying to know and love. The hymn starts with these words:
Brightly beams our father’s mercy
From his lighthouse ever more
But to us, he gives the keeping
of the lights along the shore.
In one of the areas where I served on my mission, in my third hour of lying on the floor in the middle of the day, I reencountered a Facebook reel that I know I had seen sometime before my mission. I was lying on the hardwood floor with my own dirty clothes as a pillow because of a depressive episode, I guess. I was still wearing my black nametag, white shirt, belt, and tie. The reel was a 12-second video of a tiny bubble being crushed underwater at the 4.5-second mark, and shooting beams of light off in four directions. The description had something about: “...its exact mechanism is still not fully explained. It occurs when a small gas bubbles in a liquid, excited by sound waves, collapse rapidly and emit short bursts of light….”
I was extremely lucky to serve when two incredible things happened in my mission. One was the opening of the Bangkok, Thailand temple, which would be the first in this country and now the center of the largest temple district in the world, and the division of the mission into two. The temple had been prophesied by church president Gordon B. Hinckley a few decades ago, and it was finally going to be finished when I had been in country for about a year already. I was overwhelmed and honored by the diverse ways that I was asked to help and prepare for the occasion. I handed out flyers along the streets to anyone and everyone who would take them. Sometimes that happened hundreds of miles away from the temple. It must have been so random to the mom selling fried bananas who would had never been to Bangkok. I was told that I was being an inconvenience by the สองแถว (truck) driver, who was just trying to get through his route on time.
There were excited people, though. They would meet with us on Sundays. Sometimes it was on one floor of an office building next to a sketchy hotel, sometimes it was in an actual church building that the members poured their gallons of sweat into. (meaning that it was very hot, not that they used their sweat to construct the building) Despite the sweat and social and emotional pressure, I know I felt something special in each of those buildings. I went almost every Saturday to clean them, keep them. Like lights upon a shore.
Dark the night of sin has settled
Loud the angry billows roar
Eager eyes are watching, longing
For the lights along the shore
There were many times that I felt crushed. While I was in one city, a place that felt desolate, some might say. It felt like I was meeting only people who would prefer that I didn’t exist, I was thrust even deeper into the lonely darkness. Asleep in my room, someone took my bag from right in front of my face at the base of the steel-frame bunkbed. They took the bag from the second floor and placed it on a table near the front door of the first floor, sans monetary content. This would be enough to scare anyone, I think. But God’s plan got even darker for me.
After another week, or two maybe, for some reason I can't remember the timeframe, it happened again. The first time, it was just most of the money from the one bag I carried with me all day, every day. The second time, though, I had wedged the bag in between my mattress and the cynderblock wall. The person would have had to lean over my unconscious body to yank the bag out from around me. This second time, the bag was completely removed of cash, and money had been taken from another one of my bags as well. It felt like they also stole my ability to breathe. I could never protect a future family if I couldn't even stop myself from being robbed. Twice the night of sin had settled upon me.

I didn’t know what to do after being robbed that second time. I didn’t know who I could trust. I didn’t even know if the ground could be trusted to stay closed. Sometimes I wanted it to open and swallow me up like the scriptures talk about. It felt dark and cold for a lot longer than 3 days as we walked along highways thundering with cars and busses and trains. But I was on my mission. I had no other choice but to keep getting up every day. I wondered if anyone else felt this dark.
In another city, closer to the end of my mission (sometimes referred to as a mission trip), I met someone whom I wanted to be my friend. His name was Pond. He showed up for our free English classes. He was usually reserved but cheerful, and wore bright colors. One day, he wasn’t. This time, it was a dark black hoodie and visible pools of water along the inside of his eyes. He sat in the front of the class behind the whiteboard, which was weirdly the most secluded area. I asked him multiple times what was wrong, but he refused to say anything. I only remember that he said the word มืด (dark) multiple times. After class that day, I asked if I could share a message with him about a fourteen-year-old boy. That fourteen year old boy was named Joseph Smith. He had been circled about in darkness. From that darkness he was saved by two personages: The Father and The Son. After sharing, I hugged him. I didn’t know what else to do. All I knew was that I felt like he was my brother and that I hated that darkness feeling. It is the only time I remember hugging anyone on my mission.
3 Nephi 8:
22 And there was not any light seen, neither fire, nor glimmer, neither the sun, nor the moon, nor the stars, for so great were the mists of darkness which were upon the face of the land.
Let the lower lights be burning
Send a gleam across the wave
Some poor, fainting, struggling seaman
You may rescue, you may save.
While I was still in the same city where I had been robbed, I got a chance to help a friend who was preparing for the opening of the temple. All they needed was a white, foldable clothes hamper from IKEA. For some reason, my companion and I were the people who would be able to get that for them. It took about an hour to get from the IKEA to the temple. The sky was grey and dark that whole day we were delivered it.
I had not been to any temple of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day saints for about a year. This temple was situated right in the middle of one of the busiest parts of the city. It was enclosed by two constantly busy roads. A steam train track and an electric skytrain track covered a third side of it. It may not be possible to find a noisier spot to build a sacred place. Despite these four cacophonies crushing in and the grey clouds smothering from above, the second that I stepped into the gates of the temple, and it all went quiet. I remembered something. I could be a bubble.
I have felt life tossing me back and forth like a little boat on the ocean, waves crushing down on me in the icy cold night of hopelessness. At many times, I am that seaman, eager eyes, watching longing for the lights along the shore. I know for a fact that I am not the only one who has ever felt like this. But sometimes I have also had the opportunity to be the one to which was given the keeping of the lights along the shore. I missed many opportunities to be that light to others because I was too afraid of the water pushing in on my little bubble. There were luckily a few times where I stopped trying to force my own bubble to stay inflated and light was produced through me. It happens way less than it should. But I now know that its possible to be on both sides.
Sometimes you have to be on both sides. That same bubble out of water could not exist. A bubble that collapses all the time would probably break into smaller and smaller bits until it met the same fate. So there is a balance. A really really hard one to find. But as we try, I pray that we may be fortunate enough to send a gleam out into the storms of life, across the waves. A gleam so that people like Pond, like me, may be saved.
Thank you.
